# PAGE 15-17: CHAPTER 3 - WHY ACCOUNTABILITY PARTNERS FAIL

```
CHAPTER 3

Why Accountability Partners Fail
(And How to Fix Them)


THE 80% FAILURE RATE

Here's a depressing statistic:

80% of accountability partnerships fizzle out within 6 weeks.⁸

You start strong:
"Let's check in every Monday!"
"We're going to crush our goals together!"

Six weeks later:
*crickets*

What happened?


THE 4 STRUCTURAL PROBLEMS

Most accountability partnerships fail because of structural problems, not lack of motivation:

───────────────────────

PROBLEM #1: ASYMMETRIC INVESTMENT

One person cares more about the goal than the other.

Example:
• You want to lose 20 pounds (high priority)
• Your partner wants to "read more" (low priority)

When your partner skips their goal, they don't feel bad.
But you DO feel bad when you skip yours.

Result: Resentment builds. Partnership dissolves.

───────────────────────

PROBLEM #2: SOCIAL AWKWARDNESS

It's hard to call out a friend for slacking.

You: "Hey, did you work out this week?"
Friend: "Uh... no, I was busy."
You: "..."

Saying "You're slipping, you need to step up" feels confrontational.

So you don't say it.
And the accountability evaporates.

───────────────────────

PROBLEM #3: NO CONSEQUENCES

If you skip your goal, nothing happens.

Your friend might say "Aw, better luck next week!"

But there's no real cost to failure.

No financial penalty.
No social cost (beyond mild disappointment).
No loss of credibility.

Result: Easy to rationalize skipping.

───────────────────────

PROBLEM #4: INCONSISTENT CHECK-INS

"Let's check in sometime this week."

Translation: "We'll never check in."

Without a specific day and time, check-ins become optional.

"I'll text you Monday."
[Monday passes]
"Oh I forgot, I'll do it Wednesday."
[Wednesday passes]
"Next week for sure."

Result: The system falls apart.

───────────────────────


THE SOLUTION: COMMITMENT DEVICES

A "commitment device" is a pre-commitment that makes future failure more costly.

Instead of relying on willpower in the moment, you create a structure that makes success easier than failure.

Examples:

1. FINANCIAL COMMITMENT DEVICE
• You bet money you'll achieve your goal
• If you fail, the money goes to a charity you hate
• Apps: StickK.com, Beeminder

Research: Financial commitment devices increase goal completion by 33%.⁹

2. SOCIAL COMMITMENT DEVICE
• You publicly announce your goal
• If you fail, there's a social cost (loss of credibility)
• Works best in small groups (5-10 people), not large audiences

Caveat: Can backfire if announced too early (see Chapter 5).

3. TECHNOLOGICAL COMMITMENT DEVICE
• Automated check-ins (removes "I forgot" excuse)
• Data from wearables syncs automatically
• Partner gets notified if you miss a workout
• Apps: Habitica, Beeminder, Moon Ring (launching Q2 2025)


5 ACCOUNTABILITY CONTRACTS (COPY-PASTE READY)

Here are 5 contract templates you can use with an accountability partner:

───────────────────────

CONTRACT #1: THE MUTUAL COMMITMENT
(Best for: Two people with equally important goals)

"We, [Name 1] and [Name 2], commit to:

• Check-ins every [Day] at [Time] via [Method]
• [Name 1]'s goal: [Specific goal + metric]
• [Name 2]'s goal: [Specific goal + metric]
• If either person misses 2 check-ins in a row, we schedule a review call to diagnose the problem
• This contract lasts [Duration] and can be renegotiated at the end

Signed: _______________  Date: _______________"

───────────────────────

CONTRACT #2: THE FINANCIAL STAKE
(Best for: High-stakes goals you're serious about)

"I, [Name], commit to:

• Goal: [Specific goal + metric]
• Check-in schedule: [Frequency + day/time]
• Accountability partner: [Name]
• Financial stake: $[Amount] deposited with [Person/App]
• If I achieve my goal by [Date], I get my money back
• If I fail, the money goes to [Charity I hate/Partner's choice]

I understand this is legally binding.

Signed: _______________  Date: _______________"

───────────────────────

CONTRACT #3: THE ASYMMETRIC PARTNERSHIP
(Best for: One person needs accountability, the other just wants to help)

"I, [Accountability Seeker], commit to:

• Goal: [Specific goal + metric]
• Check-in schedule: [Frequency]
• I will report to [Partner Name] via [Method]

• Asking "Did you do X this week?" at every check-in
• Responding supportively (no judgment, just observation)
• Calling out patterns if I miss 3+ check-ins

This is NOT a mutual partnership. [Partner] does not have their own goal. They're just helping me.

Signed: _______________  Date: _______________"

───────────────────────

CONTRACT #4: THE GROUP ACCOUNTABILITY
(Best for: Small group of 3-6 people with similar goals)

"We, the members of [Group Name], commit to:

• Weekly check-in meetings every [Day] at [Time]
• Each person shares: (1) Last week's progress, (2) This week's commitment
• We celebrate wins and troubleshoot obstacles (no judgment)
• If someone misses 2 meetings in a row without notice, we check in privately
• New members require unanimous approval

Current members:
• [Name 1]: [Goal]
• [Name 2]: [Goal]
• [Name 3]: [Goal]

Signed: _______________  Date: _______________"

───────────────────────

CONTRACT #5: THE ESCALATING CONSEQUENCE
(Best for: People who need stronger incentives over time)

"I, [Name], commit to:

• Goal: [Specific weekly target]
• Accountability partner: [Name]
• Check-in: Every [Day] at [Time]

Consequences for missing my goal:

• Week 1: No consequence (grace period)
• Week 2: $5 to partner's choice of charity
• Week 3: $10 to partner's choice of charity
• Week 4: $20 to partner's choice of charity
• Week 5+: $50 to partner's choice of charity

(Consequences reset after 4 consecutive successful weeks)

Signed: _______________  Date: _______________"

───────────────────────


HOW TO CHOOSE THE RIGHT PARTNER

Not all accountability partners are created equal.

Good accountability partner:
✓ Takes their own goals seriously (shows they value accountability)
✓ Comfortable with direct communication (can call you out without awkwardness)
✓ Consistent and reliable (shows up for check-ins)
✓ Supportive but firm (celebrates wins, doesn't excuse failures)

Bad accountability partner:
✗ Always makes excuses for themselves (signals they'll make excuses for you)
✗ Too nice (avoids confrontation, lets you off the hook)
✗ Unreliable (forgets check-ins, doesn't respond)
✗ Judgmental (makes you feel shame instead of support)

Pro tip: Sometimes a stranger makes a better accountability partner than a friend (less social awkwardness).


RED FLAGS THAT YOUR PARTNERSHIP IS FAILING

Watch for these warning signs:

🚩 Check-ins are getting less frequent
🚩 One person always initiates (asymmetric investment)
🚩 You're both making excuses more than hitting goals
🚩 Check-ins feel like a chore instead of support
🚩 You're avoiding your partner because you didn't hit your goal

If you see 3+ red flags, schedule a review call:

"Hey, I think our accountability partnership isn't working as well as it could. Can we schedule 15 minutes to diagnose what's going wrong?"

Sometimes the fix is simple (adjust frequency, change metric).
Sometimes the partnership needs to end (and that's okay).


KEY TAKEAWAYS

✓ 80% of accountability partnerships fail within 6 weeks due to structural problems

✓ The 4 problems: Asymmetric investment, social awkwardness, no consequences, inconsistent check-ins

✓ Solution: Commitment devices (financial, social, technological)

✓ Use the 5 contract templates to formalize your partnership

✓ Choose a partner who is consistent, direct, and committed

✓ Watch for red flags and adjust early

# PAGE 18-19: CHAPTER 4 - THE FRESH START EFFECT

```
CHAPTER 4

The Fresh Start Effect:
Strategic Timing for Behavior Change


THE POWER OF TEMPORAL LANDMARKS

Quick quiz:

When are you most likely to start a new goal?

A) A random Tuesday in March
B) January 1st (New Year's Day)

If you answered B, you're not alone.

This is called the Fresh Start Effect — the psychological phenomenon where temporal landmarks (dates that feel like "new beginnings") increase motivation for behavior change.¹⁰


WHY FRESH STARTS WORK

Temporal landmarks create a psychological "clean slate."

They allow you to mentally separate your past self (who failed at goals) from your future self (who will succeed this time).

Research shows that on fresh start dates:
• Gym attendance spikes by 33%¹¹
• Google searches for "diet" increase by 82%¹²
• Goal commitment increases across all domains

But here's the key: You don't have to wait for January 1st.

You can manufacture fresh start moments throughout the year.


YOUR 2025 FRESH START CALENDAR

Here are the temporal landmarks research shows are most effective:

───────────────────────

ANNUAL FRESH STARTS
• January 1 (New Year's Day) — The strongest fresh start
• Your birthday — Personal new year
• September 1 (Back to school season) — Even if you're not in school

QUARTERLY FRESH STARTS
• April 1 (Q2 start)
• July 1 (Q3 start / summer)
• October 1 (Q4 start / fall)

MONTHLY FRESH STARTS
• The 1st of every month
• Your "personal new year" (same day each month as your birthday)

WEEKLY FRESH STARTS
• Monday (weekly reset)
• Sunday evening (planning for the week ahead)

MICRO FRESH STARTS (Manufactured)
• First day of a new job
• Moving to a new home
• After a breakup or major life change
• The day after a vacation
• Your "Day 1" declaration (just pick a date and commit)

───────────────────────


HOW TO USE FRESH STARTS STRATEGICALLY

Don't waste your fresh start moments.

Here's how to maximize them:

STEP 1: PICK YOUR FRESH START DATE

Choose one from the calendar above.

Ideal: Within the next 7 days (don't wait for "the perfect moment")

STEP 2: CREATE A FRESH START RITUAL

Make the date feel special:

• Write down your goal and why it matters
• Clean your workspace/bedroom (physical reset)
• Tell someone "Today is my Day 1"
• Take a "before" photo/measurement

The ritual makes the date more memorable (increasing commitment).

STEP 3: PLAN YOUR FIRST WEEK

Fresh start motivation fades by Week 2.

So design Week 1 to be ridiculously easy to succeed:

Example:
• Goal: Run 3× per week
• Week 1 plan: "Run for 5 minutes on Monday/Wednesday/Friday"

Why easy? You're building the habit of showing up, not peak performance.

STEP 4: SCHEDULE YOUR NEXT FRESH START

Don't rely on just one fresh start.

If you slip up in Week 3, use the next Monday as a "mini fresh start."

If you slip up in February, use March 1st.

Fresh starts aren't one-time events. They're strategic resets you can use whenever you need them.


MANUFACTURING "MINI NEW YEARS"

You don't need to wait for a calendar date.

You can create arbitrary fresh starts:

• "Today is Week 0. Next Monday is Week 1."
• "I'm doing a 30-day challenge starting [arbitrary date]."
• "Today is the day I become the type of person who [identity]."

The research shows these work almost as well as calendar fresh starts — as long as you believe it's a fresh start.¹³

The key is the mental separation:

OLD YOU (past failures) | NEW YOU (fresh start)


COMBINING FRESH STARTS WITH ACCOUNTABILITY

Fresh starts work even better when combined with public commitment.

On your fresh start date:

1. Announce your goal to someone
2. Schedule your first accountability check-in for Day 7
3. Pre-commit to what you'll do in Week 1

Example:

"Today is my Day 1. I'm committing to [goal]. I'll report back to you next Monday with my Week 1 results."

This combines:
• Fresh Start Effect (mental clean slate)
• Public Commitment (social pressure)
• Implementation Intention (specific plan)
• Accountability (check-in scheduled)


COMMON MISTAKES

❌ Waiting for January 1st (don't wait — use the next Monday)
❌ Setting huge goals on fresh starts (start small, build momentum)
❌ Not planning beyond Week 1 (motivation fades fast)
❌ Thinking fresh starts are magic (they're just a motivational boost — you still need a system)


KEY TAKEAWAYS

✓ Fresh starts create a psychological "clean slate" that increases motivation

✓ Use the 2025 Fresh Start Calendar to plan strategic goal launches

✓ Don't wait for January — use Mondays, monthly resets, or arbitrary "Day 1" declarations

✓ Make Week 1 ridiculously easy to succeed (you're building the habit of showing up)

✓ Combine fresh starts with public commitment and accountability for maximum effect


───────────────────────

# PAGE 20: CHAPTER 5 - PUBLIC VS PRIVATE COMMITMENT

```
CHAPTER 5

Public vs. Private Commitment:
When to Share Your Goals (And When to Shut Up)


THE CONVENTIONAL WISDOM

You've probably heard this advice:

"Tell everyone your goals! It'll make you accountable!"

Social media is full of public goal announcements:

"Starting my weight loss journey! Follow along!"
"Going to run a marathon this year! Wish me luck!"

But here's the problem:

The research shows that publicly announcing your goals can backfire.


THE PREMATURE SATISFACTION EFFECT

Psychologist Peter Gollwitzer (2009) found that when you announce your goal publicly, your brain gets a premature reward — the social validation of people praising you.¹⁴

"Good for you!"
"You've got this!"
"So proud of you!"

Your brain receives the dopamine hit of success... before you've actually done the work.

This reduces motivation to follow through.

You've already received the social validation. Why bother doing the hard part?

Result: People who publicly announce identity-based goals are LESS likely to achieve them than people who keep them private.¹⁴


THE NUANCE

But wait — doesn't public commitment create accountability?

Yes. But only under specific conditions.

Here's the decision tree:

───────────────────────

PUBLIC COMMITMENT WORKS WHEN:

✓ You're committing to ACTIONS (not identity)
✓ You're sharing with a small group (5-10 people, not 500)
✓ You're asking for accountability (not validation)
✓ You've already started (Day 7+, not Day 0)

PUBLIC COMMITMENT BACKFIRES WHEN:

✗ You're announcing IDENTITY ("I'm going to become a runner")
✗ You're sharing with a large audience (social media)
✗ You're seeking validation (likes, comments, praise)
✗ You're announcing on Day 0 (before building any momentum)

───────────────────────


EXAMPLES

❌ Public announcement that backfires:

"Today is Day 1 of my weight loss journey! I'm going to lose 30 pounds and become the healthiest version of myself! Wish me luck! 💪 #transformation #NewYearNewMe"

Why it backfires:
• Day 0 announcement (no momentum yet)
• Identity-based ("become the healthiest version")
• Large audience (seeking validation)
• No specific action commitment

✓ Public commitment that works:

"Week 2 update: Went to the gym 3x this week (M/W/F). Committing to the same schedule next week. [Tag 2-3 close friends who are holding me accountable]"

Why it works:
• Week 2 (already built momentum)
• Action-based (specific commitment)
• Small audience (2-3 people)
• Asking for accountability (not validation)


THE DECISION TREE

Use this flowchart to decide whether to share your goal:

───────────────────────

Q1: Is this an IDENTITY goal or an ACTION goal?

Identity ("I'm going to become a runner")
→ KEEP PRIVATE until you've already built the identity

Action ("I'm going to run 3× this week")
→ Proceed to Q2

Q2: Have you already started (Day 7+)?

No (Day 0-6)
→ KEEP PRIVATE (build momentum first)

Yes (Day 7+)
→ Proceed to Q3

Q3: Are you sharing with a small group or large audience?

Small group (5-10 people)
→ SHARE PUBLICLY (small group accountability)

Large audience (social media)
→ KEEP PRIVATE (or share only results, not intentions)

Q4: Are you seeking accountability or validation?

Accountability ("Hold me to this")
→ SHARE PUBLICLY

Validation ("Cheer me on!")
→ KEEP PRIVATE (you'll get premature satisfaction)

───────────────────────


ALTERNATIVE: THE "ACCOUNTABILITY WITHOUT ANNOUNCEMENT" STRATEGY

Want accountability without the premature satisfaction risk?

Use this approach:

1. Set your goal privately
2. Start working on it (Week 1-2)
3. After you've built momentum, share with 1-2 accountability partners (not a large audience)
4. Report results weekly (not intentions)

Example:

Week 0: Set goal privately ("I want to run 3× per week")
Week 1-2: Do it privately (build momentum)
Week 3: Text 2 friends: "I've been running 3× per week for 2 weeks. Can you check in with me every Friday to see if I hit my 3 workouts?"

This avoids premature satisfaction while still getting accountability.


KEY TAKEAWAYS

✓ Publicly announcing identity goals reduces follow-through (premature satisfaction)

✓ Public commitment to ACTIONS works (if done correctly)

✓ Use the decision tree: Identity vs action, Day 0 vs Day 7+, small group vs large audience, accountability vs validation

✓ Alternative: Build momentum privately (Week 1-2), then share with 1-2 accountability partners


───────────────────────
¹⁴ Gollwitzer, P.M., et al. (2009). "When Intentions Go Public: Does Social Reality Widen the Intention-Behavior Gap?" Psychological Science, 20(5), 612-618.

# PAGE 21-23: CHAPTER 6 - 7 STRATEGIES YOU CAN USE TODAY

```
CHAPTER 6

7 Strategies You Can Use Today
(No App Required)


You've learned the principles.

Now here are 7 actionable strategies you can implement this week.

Each strategy is backed by research.
Each includes step-by-step instructions.

Pick ONE to start. Don't try all 7 at once.


───────────────────────

STRATEGY #1: THE SUNDAY EVENING RESET
(15-minute planning ritual)

What it is:
A weekly planning ritual that increases goal completion by 42%.¹⁵

How it works:
Every Sunday evening (7-8 PM), spend 15 minutes planning your week using implementation intentions.

Step-by-step:

1. Review last week (5 min):
   • What went well?
   • What got in the way?
   • What will I adjust this week?

2. Plan this week (10 min):
   • Write 3-5 implementation intentions
   • "If it's [Day] at [Time], then I will [Action]"
   • Schedule them in your calendar

3. Commit publicly (optional):
   • Text your accountability partner: "Here's my plan for this week"

Why it works:
• Fresh Start Effect (Monday reset)
• Implementation Intentions (specific plans)
• Weekly reflection (adjust what's not working)

───────────────────────

STRATEGY #2: THE 2-MINUTE RULE
(James Clear's technique adapted for wearables)

What it is:
Make your habit so easy it takes less than 2 minutes to start.

How it works:
Instead of "I'll work out for 30 minutes," commit to "I'll put on my workout clothes."

Step-by-step:

1. Identify your real goal:
   Example: "Run 3× per week"

2. Shrink it to 2 minutes:
   "Put on running shoes and step outside"

3. Commit to ONLY the 2-minute version for Week 1

4. Once the 2-minute version is automatic (Week 2-3), gradually increase

Why it works:
• Reduces friction (2 minutes is too easy to skip)
• Builds the habit of showing up (which is 80% of the battle)
• Once you're in workout clothes outside, you'll probably just run

───────────────────────

STRATEGY #3: THE ACCOUNTABILITY TEXT CHAIN
(Group accountability without annoying your friends)

What it is:
A text chain where 3-5 people report their daily/weekly wins.

How it works:
Create a group text with 3-5 people. Every [Day] at [Time], everyone sends ONE message:

"✅ Did X today" or "❌ Missed today"

No explanations. No excuses. Just accountability.

Step-by-step:

1. Find 3-5 people with similar goals (friends, coworkers, or join an online group)

2. Create a group text

3. Set a rule: Every [Day] at [Time], send your status

4. React with emoji (👍 for wins, no judgment for misses)

5. Weekly review (optional): "Here's my week: ✅✅❌✅✅✅✅"

Why it works:
• Social observation (you don't want to be the only one missing)
• Low friction (one text, no long explanations)
• Positive reinforcement (group celebrates wins)

───────────────────────

STRATEGY #4: THE STREAK CONTRACT
(Gamification that actually works)

What it is:
Track your streak (consecutive days/weeks of hitting your goal). Longer streak = higher motivation to not break it.

How it works:
Use a simple tracker (calendar, app, or spreadsheet). Mark every day you hit your goal.

Step-by-step:

1. Define your goal clearly:
   Example: "Workout 3× per week"

2. Choose your tracker:
   • Paper calendar (mark X on successful days)
   • App: Habitica, Streaks, Way of Life
   • Spreadsheet (Google Sheets)

3. Track daily/weekly (don't skip)

4. When you hit milestones, celebrate:
   • 1-week streak: Share with accountability partner
   • 4-week streak: Treat yourself (small reward)
   • 12-week streak: Big celebration

5. If you break your streak: Use the next Monday as a fresh start (don't give up entirely)

Why it works:
• Sunk cost fallacy (the longer your streak, the more painful it is to break)
• Visual progress (seeing X's on a calendar is motivating)
• Clear feedback (you either hit it or you didn't)

Caveat: Don't get obsessed with "never breaking the streak" — perfection isn't the goal. If you miss one day, just start a new streak.

───────────────────────

STRATEGY #5: THE FAILURE PRE-MORTEM
(Plan for obstacles before they derail you)

What it is:
Before starting your goal, imagine everything that could go wrong — and plan for it.

How it works:
Ask yourself: "What are all the reasons I might fail at this goal?"

Then create "If-Then" plans for each obstacle.

Step-by-step:

1. Brainstorm obstacles (10 min):
   • What got in the way last time I tried this goal?
   • What external factors could derail me? (weather, work stress, social events)
   • What internal factors? (motivation dips, self-sabotage)

2. Create "If-Then" plans for each obstacle:

   Example obstacles & plans:

   ❌ "What if it's raining and I can't run outside?"
   ✅ "If it's raining, then I'll do a 20-min indoor workout video"

   ❌ "What if I'm too tired in the morning?"
   ✅ "If I'm too tired, then I'll do a 10-min yoga session instead"

   ❌ "What if my friend invites me out for drinks on a workout night?"
   ✅ "If I get invited out, then I'll suggest meeting after my workout"

3. Write these down and keep them visible

Why it works:
• Anticipates obstacles before they happen (reduces decision fatigue in the moment)
• Removes "I didn't plan for this" excuse
• Research: People who do pre-mortems are 30% more likely to achieve goals¹⁶

───────────────────────

STRATEGY #6: THE WEEKLY ACCOUNTABILITY EMAIL
(Self-reporting framework that mimics a coach)

What it is:
Every week, send yourself (and optionally an accountability partner) a structured report.

How it works:
Every Friday, send an email answering these 4 questions:

Step-by-step:

1. Set a weekly recurring calendar reminder: "Friday 5 PM: Weekly Accountability Email"

2. Answer these 4 questions:

   **Q1: What was my goal this week?**
   (Be specific: "Workout 3× this week")

   **Q2: Did I achieve it?**
   (Yes/No + brief explanation)

   **Q3: What got in the way?**
   (1-2 obstacles)

   **Q4: What's my plan for next week?**
   (1-2 adjustments)

3. Send to yourself (and/or accountability partner)

4. Review monthly: Read your last 4 emails. What patterns do you see?

Why it works:
• Self-reflection (forces you to confront what's actually happening)
• Weekly check-in (consistent accountability)
• Pattern recognition (monthly review reveals what's consistently getting in the way)

Bonus: If you send this to an accountability partner, they can reply with observations ("I've noticed you always skip Thursday workouts — what's happening on Thursdays?")

───────────────────────

STRATEGY #7: THE "TOO EASY TO FAIL" CHALLENGE
(Reverse engineering success)

What it is:
Make your goal SO easy that it's harder to skip than to do.

How it works:
Instead of "I'll work out for 30 minutes," commit to "I'll do 1 pushup."

Seriously. Just one.

Step-by-step:

1. Take your real goal and shrink it to the smallest possible version

   Examples:
   • "Write 1,000 words" → "Write 1 sentence"
   • "Meditate for 10 minutes" → "Take 3 deep breaths"
   • "Run 3 miles" → "Put on running shoes and step outside"

2. Commit to ONLY the tiny version for 7 days

3. Tell yourself: "I can stop after 1 pushup/1 sentence/3 breaths"

4. Most days, you'll keep going (but you don't have to)

Why it works:
• Removes the friction of starting (1 pushup is laughably easy)
• Most days, starting leads to continuing ("Well, I'm already doing pushups, might as well do 10")
• Builds the habit of showing up (even on low-motivation days)
• Zero-guilt on days you only do the minimum

Research: "Too easy to fail" habits have an 85% adherence rate (vs 35% for normal-sized habits).¹⁷

───────────────────────


WHICH STRATEGY SHOULD YOU START WITH?

Pick based on your biggest obstacle:

• **Problem: I keep forgetting to do it**
  → Try: Strategy #1 (Sunday Evening Reset) or Strategy #6 (Weekly Accountability Email)

• **Problem: I start strong but quit after 2 weeks**
  → Try: Strategy #2 (2-Minute Rule) or Strategy #7 (Too Easy to Fail)

• **Problem: I lack motivation**
  → Try: Strategy #3 (Accountability Text Chain) or Strategy #4 (Streak Contract)

• **Problem: Unexpected obstacles derail me**
  → Try: Strategy #5 (Failure Pre-Mortem)

Don't try all 7 at once.

Pick ONE. Do it for 4 weeks. Then add another if needed.


KEY TAKEAWAYS

✓ These 7 strategies are backed by research and require no app

✓ Pick ONE strategy to start (don't overwhelm yourself)

✓ Combine strategies over time (e.g., Sunday Reset + Accountability Text Chain)

✓ Adjust based on what's working (use the weekly review to diagnose)
