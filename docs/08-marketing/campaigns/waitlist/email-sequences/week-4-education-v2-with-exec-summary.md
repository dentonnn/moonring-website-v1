**PRINCIPLE #1: THE "IF-THEN" FORMULA**

Research by Peter Gollwitzer (2006) found that people who use "implementation intentions" are 2-3× more likely to follow through on goals.

What's an implementation intention?

Instead of:
❌ "I'll exercise more"

Try:
✅ "If it's Monday/Wednesday/Friday at 6 AM, then I'll go to the gym for 30 minutes"

The specific trigger ("If it's Monday at 6 AM") removes decision-making friction.

**Your brain doesn't have to decide** — it just executes the pre-programmed plan.

**PRINCIPLE #2: THE GOLDILOCKS ZONE**

Too little accountability = no effect.
Too much accountability = rebellion.

The research reveals there's an optimal "sweet spot."

**The Data:**

Under-Accountable (12% completion):
"Tell me your goal whenever you feel like it"

Over-Accountable (18% completion):
"I'll check in daily and you must report exact metrics"
→ People avoid/resent this

Goldilocks Zone (58% completion):
"Choose 2-3 specific check-ins per week with pre-defined metrics"

What makes accountability "just right"?
• Frequency (not too much, not too little)
• Autonomy (you choose the metrics)
• Social observation (not judgment)
• Positive reinforcement (not shame)

**PRINCIPLE #3: WHY ACCOUNTABILITY PARTNERS FAIL**

80% of accountability partnerships fizzle out within 6 weeks.

Why?

**Structural problems:**

1. Asymmetric investment — One person cares more
2. Social awkwardness — Hard to call out a friend
3. No consequences — If you skip, nothing happens
4. Inconsistent check-ins — "Let's check in sometime" = never

**The Fix: Commitment Devices**

A "commitment device" is a pre-commitment that makes future failure more costly.

Examples:
• Betting money you'll lose if you don't follow through (StickK.com model)
• Public commitment (social cost of failing)
• Automated check-ins (removes "I forgot" excuse)

Research: Commitment devices increase goal completion by 33% (Kaur et al., 2015).

**In the full PDF:** You'll get 5 accountability "contracts" you can use with a partner + templates for setting up commitment devices (Page 15-17).
