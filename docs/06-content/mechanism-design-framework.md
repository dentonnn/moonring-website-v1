## Part 1: Mechanism Design Fundamentals

### What is Mechanism Design?

**Simple Definition:** Mechanism design is "reverse game theory" - instead of predicting how people behave under existing rules, you design rules that make people's selfish actions produce outcomes you want.

**Nobel Prize Insight (2007):**
Leonid Hurwicz, Eric Maskin, and Roger Myerson won the Nobel Prize for proving that you can engineer systems where self-interest serves collective good. The key: align individual incentives with desired outcomes.

**Traditional Fitness Apps vs. Mechanism Design:**

| Traditional Approach | Mechanism Design Approach |
|---------------------|---------------------------|
| Rely on intrinsic motivation | Engineer extrinsic incentives |
| Hope users stay committed | Make commitment the rational choice |
| Track data passively | Verify effort objectively |
| Individual willpower | Social accountability systems |
| Rewards for success | Penalties for failure (loss aversion) |

### Why Fitness Apps Fail: The Commitment Problem

**The Core Issue:** 30% of fitness wearables are abandoned within 6 months despite perfect data collection. Why?

**Behavioral Economics Explanation:**
Humans suffer from "present bias" - we over-value immediate pleasure (skipping workout, eating dessert) and under-value future benefits (being fit, healthy). Traditional apps assume you'll overcome this bias through willpower alone. Mechanism design recognizes willpower fails predictably.

**The Mechanism Design Solution:**
Change the incentive structure so that immediate consequences favor healthy behaviors. If skipping your workout means:
- Losing $20 (financial stake)
- Disappointing your accountability partner (social cost)
- Losing streak badges (status loss)
- Public failure notification (reputation damage)

...then suddenly, doing the workout becomes the path of least resistance. **This isn't motivation - it's incentive engineering.**

### The Three Pillars of Mechanism Design

**1. Incentive Compatibility**
Design systems where telling the truth and following through is the best strategy. Moon Ring achieves this through wearable verification - you can't lie about completing your workout when your Apple Watch data is checked.

**2. Individual Rationality**
Participants must benefit from participation, or they won't join. Moon Ring offers clear value: accountability partners, community support, behavioral psychology coaching - making participation attractive despite the commitment required.

**3. Budget Balance**
The system must be sustainable. Moon Ring's peer accountability model scales efficiently - as community grows, partner matching improves without proportional cost increases. User-to-user accountability creates value without requiring massive infrastructure.

### Real-World Mechanism Design Examples

**Kidney Exchange Programs:**
Patients needing transplants have willing donors who aren't compatible matches. Mechanism design creates exchange chains where A's donor gives to B, B's donor gives to C, etc. Everyone gets what they need by cooperating within structured rules.

**Auction Theory:**
Google's AdWords auctions use mechanism design - bidders compete for ad placement, and the system extracts maximum value while ensuring advertisers get relevant traffic. Self-interest (getting clicks) produces optimal ad placement for users.

**Environmental Markets:**
Cap-and-trade systems let companies buy/sell pollution credits. Firms that can reduce emissions cheaply do so and sell credits to companies where reduction is expensive. Self-interest (profit) produces emission reductions.

**Moon Ring's Application:**
Users seeking fitness results join accountability partnerships. Those strong in areas where you're weak become partners, creating mutual monitoring. Self-interest (achieving your goals, avoiding loss) produces community-wide health improvements.

## Part 2: Proof-of-Work Analogy - Verified Effort

### Understanding Blockchain Proof-of-Work

**Bitcoin's Innovation:**
Before Bitcoin, digital currencies faced the "double-spending problem" - how do you prevent someone from spending the same digital coin twice? Bitcoin solved this with Proof-of-Work: miners must expend real computational energy to validate transactions. Cheating is prohibitively expensive.

**The Core Mechanism:**
1. Miners compete to solve complex mathematical puzzles
2. Solving requires significant electricity and computing power (real cost)
3. First to solve gets to add block to blockchain and earn rewards
4. Other miners verify the solution (easy to check, hard to produce)
5. Attacking the network requires more energy than honest participation costs

**Key Insight:** Making lies expensive prevents fraud. Honest work is the economically rational choice.

### Moon Ring's Proof-of-Work: Wearable Verification

**The Fitness Equivalent:**
Traditional fitness apps rely on self-reporting: "Did you work out today? Yes/No." Mechanism design recognizes this creates incentive to lie. Moon Ring solves this with wearable verification - your Apple Watch, Fitbit, or Oura Ring provides cryptographic proof of physical effort.

**How It Works:**

**1. Objective Data Collection**
- Wearables track steps, heart rate, sleep, activity minutes
- Data syncs automatically to Moon Ring platform
- No manual entry = no opportunity to inflate results

**2. Commitment Verification**
- Goal: "Walk 10,000 steps daily for 30 days"
- Wearable data proves whether goal achieved each day
- Verification happens automatically at day's end

**3. Falsification Prevention**
- Can't fake wearable data (device-level encryption)
- Anomaly detection flags impossible metrics
- Community can challenge suspicious achievements

**4. Cost of Cheating**
- Buying steps (treadmill while working) still requires real effort
- Shaking device to generate steps detected by accelerometer patterns
- Getting someone else to wear your device violates commitment contract

**Real-World Parallel:**
Just as Bitcoin miners can't fake computational work, Moon Ring users can't fake physical work. The verification mechanism makes honesty the only viable strategy.

### Why Proof-of-Work Matters for Accountability

**Problem:** Self-reported goals fail because humans are excellent at self-deception. "I basically worked out" when you walked to the fridge. "I'm eating healthy" while consuming 3,000 calories. Vague goals create loopholes.

**Solution:** Objective verification removes ambiguity. Either your wearable shows 10,000 steps or it doesn't. No debate, no rationalization, no wiggle room.

**Psychological Impact:**

**Before Verification (Traditional Apps):**
- "I'll count today as a rest day" (every day)
- "I basically hit my goal" (at 60% completion)
- "The app must be wrong" (when it shows failure)

**After Verification (Moon Ring):**
- Data shows 7,200 steps → commitment not met → consequence triggered
- No mental gymnastics available
- Forced confrontation with reality

**The Accountability Multiplier:**
When your accountability partner sees the same verified data, social pressure compounds. You can't fool yourself AND your partner. The combination of objective proof and social monitoring creates accountability that's 91% effective vs. 65% for solo efforts.

### Proof-of-Work Scaling: Graduated Commitments

**Bitcoin Mining Difficulty Adjustment:**
As more miners join, Bitcoin automatically increases puzzle difficulty to maintain 10-minute block times. This prevents inflation while rewarding increased network security.

**Moon Ring's Adaptive Challenge:**
As you succeed at commitments, Moon Ring increases difficulty to maintain growth:

**Level 1 (Beginner):** Walk 5,000 steps daily - Low stake, high support
**Level 2 (Intermediate):** Walk 10,000 steps daily - Medium stake, peer accountability
**Level 3 (Advanced):** Walk 15,000 steps + 3 workouts weekly - High stake, community status

**Blockchain Perspective:**
Bitcoin's security relies on energy being expensive. Attacking the network requires more electricity than honest mining, making attacks economically irrational.

**Fitness Perspective:**
Moon Ring's effectiveness relies on physical effort being limited. You can't walk 10,000 steps without expending time and calories. This scarcity makes commitments meaningful.

**The Verification Advantage:**

| Without Verification | With Wearable Proof-of-Work |
|---------------------|----------------------------|
| "I worked out" | Heart rate data shows 45 min in cardio zone |
| "I slept 8 hours" | Sleep tracking shows 7:42 actual sleep |

**Commitment Credibility:**
When you stake $50 on walking 10,000 steps daily, wearable verification makes the commitment credible. Others know you can't cheat. This credibility enables social accountability - partners trust your goals are real, making their support meaningful.

## Part 3: Proof-of-Stake Analogy - Commitment Staking

### Understanding Blockchain Proof-of-Stake

**The Innovation:**
Ethereum 2.0 and other modern blockchains use Proof-of-Stake instead of energy-intensive mining. Validators "stake" (lock up) cryptocurrency to earn the right to validate transactions. If they cheat or fail, they lose their stake.

**How It Works:**
1. Validator deposits 32 ETH ($50,000+) as collateral
2. Network randomly selects validators to propose blocks
3. Honest validation earns rewards (additional ETH)
4. Malicious behavior = "slashing" (losing staked ETH)
5. Rational validators act honestly to protect their investment

**Key Insight:** People protect what they've invested. Making dishonesty expensive creates honest behavior.

### Moon Ring's Proof-of-Stake: Commitment Staking

**The Fitness Equivalent:**
Instead of staking cryptocurrency, Moon Ring users stake three forms of capital when setting goals:

**1. Financial Capital (Money Stakes)**

- Fail commitment = forfeit stake (donated to charity or partner)
- Higher stakes for users needing stronger incentives

**2. Social Capital (Reputation Stakes)**
- Commitment visible to accountability partner and community
- Success builds status, unlocks leadership opportunities
- Failure damages credibility, reduces partner matching quality
- Streak counters showcase consistency publicly

**3. Relational Capital (Partnership Stakes)**
- Accountability partner invested in your success
- Your failure disappoints someone counting on you
- Mutual commitments create reciprocal obligations
- Breaking promises damages relationship equity

**Mechanism Design Principle:**
By requiring users to "stake" something valuable (money, reputation, relationships), Moon Ring ensures commitment credibility. Like PoS validators protecting their ETH deposit, Moon Ring users protect their stakes by following through.

### Why Staking Works: Loss Aversion

Nobel laureate Daniel Kahneman proved humans experience losses 2x more intensely than equivalent gains. Losing $20 hurts twice as much as gaining $20 feels good.

## Part 4: Game Theory and Commitment Contracts

### The Fundamental Commitment Problem

**Game Theory Definition:**
Humans have "time-inconsistent preferences" - what we want in the future differs from what we want now. Future-you wants to be fit. Present-you wants to skip the gym.

**The Paradox:**
- At 6am Monday: "I'll definitely work out after work all week"
- At 6pm Monday: "I'm too tired, I'll start tomorrow"
- At 6am Tuesday: "This week I'll really do it"
- At 6pm Tuesday: "One more day won't hurt"

This isn't weakness - it's predictable human behavior. Game theory proves isolated individuals systematically fail at inter-temporal commitments (promises to your future self).

### Nash Equilibrium: Why Solo Goals Fail

**Game Theory Concept:**
A Nash Equilibrium is a stable state where no player can improve outcomes by changing strategy alone. In solo fitness:

**The Solo Fitness Game:**
- **Strategy A:** Work out consistently (effort required)
- **Strategy B:** Skip workouts (immediate comfort)

**Payoff Analysis:**
- Strategy A: -5 happiness (workout discomfort) + 2 happiness (progress) = -3 net
- Strategy B: +3 happiness (relaxation) + 0 progress = +3 net

**Nash Equilibrium: Skip workouts**
In isolation, skipping is the dominant strategy. The immediate comfort (+3) outweighs delayed progress reward (+2). This explains why 65% of solo fitness goals fail - the equilibrium favors inaction.

**The Mechanism Design Flaw:**
Traditional fitness apps don't change the payoff structure. Adding points or badges (small delayed rewards) can't overcome immediate comfort preference.

### The Accountability Partnership: Changing the Game

**Moon Ring's Mechanism:**
Introduce a second player (accountability partner) who monitors your behavior. Now the game matrix changes:

**The Partnership Game:**

| Your Action | Partner Cooperates | Partner Defects |
|-------------|-------------------|-----------------|
| **Work out** | You: +2, Partner: +2 | You: -2, Partner: +3 |
| **Skip workout** | You: +3, Partner: -2 | You: 0, Partner: 0 |

**New Payoffs:**
- **Work out + Partner works out:** +2 (mutual success, social reward)
- **Work out + Partner skips:** -2 (you're the chump who tried)
- **Skip + Partner works out:** +3 (free ride on their effort? No - they notice and judge you)
- **Both skip:** 0 (mutual failure, relationship damaged)

**New Nash Equilibrium: Mutual cooperation**
When both players work out, neither can improve by deviating. Skipping while partner succeeds creates social cost (-2) exceeding comfort gain (+3). The equilibrium shifts to cooperation.

**Why This Works:**
Humans are social creatures with deep aversion to letting others down (social pain) and being judged as lazy (reputation damage). Adding a second player transforms the incentive landscape from "comfort vs. effort" to "social approval vs. social shame."

### The Prisoner's Dilemma and Repeated Games

**Classic Prisoner's Dilemma:**
Two criminals are interrogated separately. Each faces:
- Cooperate (stay silent): Both get 1 year if both silent, 10 years if partner talks
- Defect (confess): Go free if partner stays silent, 5 years if both talk

**Nash Equilibrium:** Both confess (defect) - safest strategy since you can't trust partner.

**Fitness Application:**
Traditional workout buddies face this dilemma:
- Both show up: Good workout for both
- Partner doesn't show: You wasted time coming
- You don't show: Save time, partner wasted theirs
- Both skip: No workout but no wasted trip

**Problem:** Without commitment mechanisms, defection (skipping) is rational since you can't trust partner. This is why casual workout buddies fail - the equilibrium is mutual defection.

### Repeated Games and Reputation: The Moon Ring Solution

**Game Theory Insight:**
The Prisoner's Dilemma changes dramatically when games repeat indefinitely. Strategies like "Tit-for-Tat" (cooperate first, then mirror partner's previous action) can sustain cooperation.

**Moon Ring's Repeated Game Design:**

**1. Indefinite Horizon**
- Partnerships aren't one-time (like casual gym buddies)
- Commitments run 30+ days with renewal options
- Long-term relationships build over months/years

**2. Reputation Tracking**
- Each interaction logged publicly
- Partner rating system (5 stars)
- Abandonment flagged on profile
- Future partner quality depends on reputation

**3. Tit-for-Tat Enforcement**
- Partner skips → you get notification + chance to respond
- Repeated skipping → partner match dissolved, reputation damaged
- Consistent showing up → partner reciprocates, relationship strengthens

**The Mechanism:**
By making the "game" repeat indefinitely and tracking reputation, Moon Ring transforms short-term incentives. Defecting today means:
- Partner may defect tomorrow (reciprocity)
- Reputation damaged (future partners see failure)
- Relationship equity destroyed (months of trust gone)

**Nash Equilibrium in Repeated Games:** Sustained cooperation.

Defection's one-time benefit (skipping workout) can't justify long-term costs (damaged reputation, lost partnerships, reduced future support). Cooperation becomes the dominant strategy.

### Commitment Devices: Ulysses Contracts

**Historical Example:**
In Homer's Odyssey, Ulysses knows he'll be tempted by Sirens' deadly song. His solution: Order crew to tie him to the ship's mast and ignore his future commands. Present-Ulysses binds Future-Ulysses to prevent predictable failure.

**Modern Application:**
Commitment devices are pre-commitments that restrict future choices:
- Alcoholic throws away all bottles before temptation strikes
- Writer uses app that blocks social media during work hours
- Dieter has friend hide junk food and only reveal on cheat day

**Moon Ring as Ultimate Commitment Device:**

**Pre-Commitment Mechanisms:**

**1. Financial Binding**
You stake $50 on 10,000 daily steps. Once staked, you cannot:

- Cancel without forfeiting penalty
- Claim you "forgot" (wearable proves otherwise)

**2. Social Binding**
You announce goal to accountability partner and community. Once public, you face:
- Partner checking in daily (can't hide)

By binding your future self through financial, social, and technical mechanisms, Moon Ring solves the commitment problem. Present-you makes the rational commitment, Future-you cannot defect even when tempted.

### Coordination Games: Community Challenges

**Coordination Game Structure:**
Unlike Prisoner's Dilemma (where defection is individually rational), coordination games reward matching others' actions:

**Example: Team Fitness Challenge**
- If most team works out → you should too (team wins, you contribute)
- If most team skips → you might as well skip (team already lost)

**Two Equilibria:**
1. Everyone participates (good equilibrium)
2. No one participates (bad equilibrium)

**The Challenge:** Groups can get stuck in bad equilibria even when good equilibria exist.

**Moon Ring's Coordination Mechanism:**

**1. Commitment Momentum**
- Early team participants create social pressure
- First 3 people commit → 4th person more likely
- Cascading commitments push toward good equilibrium

**2. Visibility and Peer Pressure**
- Team dashboard shows who's participating
- Public commitment creates obligation
- Non-participants visibly let team down

**3. Tipping Point Design**
- Challenges require minimum participation (e.g., 60% of team)
- Once threshold reached, social pressure intensifies
- Non-participants become obvious outliers

**4. Reward Structure**
- Bonus rewards if 100% team participation
- Individual rewards even if team fails (personal accountability)
- Extra penalties for being the "defector" who caused team failure

**The Mechanism:**
By making participation visible, creating tipping point incentives, and rewarding collective success, Moon Ring pushes groups toward good equilibrium (everyone participates). Once momentum builds, defecting becomes socially costly - the opposite of Prisoner's Dilemma.

## Part 5: Behavioral Economics and Commitment Devices

### Loss Aversion: The 2X Motivation Multiplier

**Kahneman-Tversky Discovery:**
Prospect Theory (Nobel Prize 2002) proved humans are loss-averse: losing $100 causes ~2x more pain than gaining $100 causes pleasure.

**Traditional Fitness Apps (Gain Framing):**
- "Earn 50 points for working out!" → Mild positive emotion
- "Unlock achievement badge!" → Brief satisfaction
- "Reach new level!" → Temporary status boost

**Problem:** Gains are nice but not compelling. You can skip today and earn points tomorrow. No urgency, no loss.

**Moon Ring (Loss Framing):**
- "Lose $20 if you skip workout" → Immediate pain anticipation
- "Break 47-day streak" → Loss of accumulated investment
- "Disappoint accountability partner" → Social relationship damage
- "Forfeit leader status" → Reputation loss

**Impact:** 2x motivation because losses hurt twice as much. The threat of losing $20 provides stronger motivation than the promise of earning $20.

### Reference Point Manipulation

**Behavioral Economics Insight:**
People evaluate outcomes relative to reference points (expectations), not absolute terms. The same objective outcome feels like gain or loss depending on framing.

**Example:**
- Expect to lose $50, actually lose $30 → feels like $20 gain
- Expect to gain $30, actually gain $10 → feels like $20 loss

**Moon Ring's Reference Point Engineering:**

**1. Stake Framing**
- Option A: "Pay $20/month subscription" → feels like ongoing cost
- Option B: "Deposit $20, recover if successful" → feels like temporary hold
- Same $20, different perception

**2. Progress Framing**
- Traditional: "You've achieved 70% of goal" → 30% failure visible
- Moon Ring: "You've earned back $14 of $20 stake" → concrete value gained
- Same progress, different emotional impact

**3. Streak Framing**
- Traditional: "You worked out 15 times" → abstract count
- Moon Ring: "You've invested 15 days, don't lose it" → accumulated value at risk
- Same behavior, different loss aversion trigger

**The Mechanism:**
By framing stakes as deposits to recover (not fees to pay), progress as money earned (not just tasks completed), and streaks as investments at risk (not just numbers), Moon Ring leverages reference points to maximize motivation through loss aversion.

### Hyperbolic Discounting: Why Future-You Fails

**The Problem:**
Humans use hyperbolic discounting: we heavily discount future rewards, making immediate gratification irresistible.

**Mathematical Demonstration:**
- Reward today: 100% value
- Reward in 1 week: ~70% value
- Reward in 1 month: ~50% value
- Reward in 3 months: ~25% value

**Fitness Failure:**
- Skip workout today: +10 immediate comfort
- Being fit in 3 months: +40 value × 25% discount = +10 perceived value
- Result: Skipping and fitness are equal → tie goes to easier option (skipping)

**Traditional Apps:**
Try to increase future value (better badges, more points) but can't overcome discounting. Future rewards will always be discounted.

**Moon Ring's Solution: Immediate Consequences**

Instead of increasing future rewards, create immediate consequences:

**Immediate Loss Architecture:**
1. Skip workout today → Lose $5 TODAY (stake partial forfeit)
2. Partner gets notification TODAY (social shame immediate)
3. Streak breaks TODAY (visible loss instant)
4. Leaderboard rank drops TODAY (status loss immediate)

**New Decision Calculus:**
- Skip workout: +10 comfort - 15 immediate losses = -5 net
- Do workout: -8 effort + 2 progress + 15 avoided losses = +9 net

**Result:** By making consequences immediate rather than delayed, Moon Ring overcomes hyperbolic discounting. Present-you experiences the costs of failure, not just Future-you.

### The Endowment Effect: Owning Your Commitment

**Behavioral Economics Finding:**
People value things more once they own them. Students given coffee mugs demand 2x more to sell them than non-owners will pay to buy identical mugs.

**Moon Ring's Ownership Engineering:**

**1. Streak Ownership**
- After 10-day streak, you "own" the achievement
- Breaking it feels like losing property you possess
- Longer streak = greater ownership feeling = stronger protection motivation

**2. Reputation Ownership**
- Build 85 commitment score over 6 months
- Score becomes part of identity ("I'm an 85")
- Losing points feels like property theft, not just number change

**3. Partnership Equity**
- Invest 30 days building accountability partnership
- Relationship becomes valuable asset you own
- Abandoning partner feels like destroying owned property

**4. Community Status**
- Earn leadership role, featured story, mentor position
- Status becomes possession, part of self-concept
- Losing status through failure = losing owned asset

**The Mechanism:**
By creating assets users accumulate over time (streaks, reputation, relationships, status), Moon Ring triggers endowment effect. Users fiercely protect these "possessions" - the thought of losing them creates powerful motivation to maintain commitment.

### Social Proof and Herd Behavior

**Behavioral Economics Principle:**
Humans look to others' behavior to decide correct action, especially under uncertainty. "If everyone else is doing it, it must be right."

**Traditional Fitness Isolation:**
- Work out alone → no social proof
- Unclear if effort is normal → doubt creeps in
- Skip workout → no one notices → seems acceptable

**Moon Ring's Social Proof Engineering:**

**Pitfall 4: Motivation Fatigue (Burnout After 3 Months)**

**Mechanism Failure:** Mechanisms create sustainable commitment, but humans need rest cycles.

**Solution:**
- Scheduled "sabbaticals" (maintain reputation while taking 2-week break)
- Reduced-intensity goals during recovery periods
- Partner rotation option (fresh relationship energy)
- Shift to mentoring role (giving support instead of receiving creates different engagement)

**Pitfall 5: Network Isolation (Small Community)**

**Problem:** Early-stage user in niche demographic (e.g., night shift worker) can't find compatible partners.

**Mechanism Failure:** Network effects require critical mass, small networks provide limited value.

**Solution:**
- Manual matching by community managers in early stages
- Broader matching criteria (geographic/schedule flexibility)
- Asynchronous partnership options (not real-time check-ins)
- Recruit-a-friend incentives to build niche subcommunities

### Ethical Considerations and Mechanism Limitations

**Mechanism Design Power = Responsibility**

**Concern 1: Exploitation of Cognitive Biases**

**Critique:** "Moon Ring weaponizes loss aversion, sunk cost fallacy, social pressure - isn't this manipulation?"

**Response:**
- Transparent design: Users understand mechanisms before opting in
- Opt-in participation: No one forced to use these systems
- Beneficial outcomes: Unlike predatory gambling, mechanisms produce health improvements
- User autonomy: Can exit anytime, keep all learning and relationships

**Ethical Framework:**
Mechanisms are tools. Using loss aversion to sell harmful products = unethical. Using loss aversion to help people achieve their stated health goals = ethical application of behavioral science.

**Concern 2: Excessive Pressure and Mental Health**

**Critique:** "Constant accountability, financial stakes, social monitoring could trigger anxiety, eating disorders, exercise addiction."

**Response:**
- Graduated intensity: Start low-pressure, user controls escalation
- Mental health screening: Identify vulnerable users, recommend professional support
- Circuit breakers: Sabbatical options, stake refunds for health issues
- Professional integration: Therapists can supervise high-risk users

**Ethical Framework:**
Mechanisms should enhance wellbeing, not create new pathologies. Requires monitoring for adverse effects, prioritizing mental health over commitment completion.

**Concern 3: Privacy and Data Exploitation**

**Critique:** "Detailed health data, behavioral patterns, social relationships - massive surveillance potential."

**Response:**
- Data minimization: Collect only commitment-relevant data
- User ownership: Individuals control data sharing, deletion
- Encryption: Health data encrypted at rest and in transit
- No data selling: Business model is subscriptions, not data monetization

**Ethical Framework:**
Verification mechanisms require data, but users must retain ownership and control. Trust is earned through privacy protection, not just mechanism effectiveness.

**Concern 4: Inequality and Access**

**Critique:** "Financial stakes favor wealthy users. Wearable verification requires expensive devices. This is accountability for the privileged."

**Response:**
- Sliding scale stakes: $5 commitment meaningful to low-income users, $500 to high-income
- Device equity: Partner with manufacturers for discounted wearables, accept smartphone tracking
- Free tier: Basic accountability features available without payment
- Corporate subsidy: Employers cover costs for employee wellness programs

**Ethical Framework:**
Mechanism design should be inclusive. While some features may have barriers, core accountability mechanisms must be accessible regardless of income.
